Computer modeling of acrylic acid–choline chloride deep eutectic solvent associates using density functional theory.
The objective of this thesis is to perform quantum-chemical modeling—using density functional theory—of an associate formed by acrylic acid and choline chloride, in order to establish a correlation between its structure, the energetics of its intermolecular interactions, and its propensity to form a deep eutectic solvent.
To achieve this objective, the following tasks must be addressed:
1. Analyze the literature regarding quantum-chemical calculation methods for intermolecular interactions in deep eutectic solvents. 
2. Optimize the geometry of the individual molecules (acrylic acid and choline chloride) as well as their complexes with varying stoichiometries. 
3. Calculate the intermolecular interaction energies and hydrogen-bonding parameters for the complexes under study. 
4. Identify the most energetically favorable component ratios and compare these findings with existing literature data. 
5. Propose recommendations for the optimal composition of the eutectic mixture based on the results obtained.
